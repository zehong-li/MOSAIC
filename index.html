Overview

Training-free perceptual quality assessment for point clouds.

MOSAIC organizes full-reference quality evidence as interpretable perceptual cues instead of fitting a dataset-specific score regressor.

MOSAIC formulates full-reference point cloud quality assessment as a multimodal perceptual inference problem. Inspired by Marr's three-level account of vision, it predicts perceived quality from complementary discrepancies in 3D geometry, 3D color, and projected 2D saliency.

The framework measures intrinsic fidelity cues in the native 3D domain, then incorporates attention-related saliency consistency through a two-stage adaptive fusion strategy. It does not rely on MOS-supervised fitting or regression-based score integration.

Method

3D structure and appearance first, saliency-aware fusion second.

The pipeline keeps the native point-cloud comparison visible, then adds view-dependent attention as a separate perceptual modulation stage.

Geometry

3D Geometry

Local shape, linearity, curvature, and density cues capture structural fidelity between reference and distorted point clouds.

Color

3D Color

RGB color contrast models native appearance consistency without moving the core comparison into a learned feature space.

Saliency

2D Saliency

Multi-view projection and saliency detection model attention-related visual importance during rendered inspection.

Fusion

Adaptive Fusion

A two-stage strategy first integrates intrinsic 3D fidelity, then modulates it with saliency consistency.
